RoPE — A Geometry Explainer

What rotary position embedding bounds, and what it cannot tell you. It is not a prefetch signal.

RoPE cannot drive prefetch — the hit-rate claim on this figure is retracted

RoPE is a rotation applied to Q/K at compute time. The rotation is a function of position alone: known in advance for every position, and identical whether a block sits in HBM or on NVMe. It therefore carries no information about which blocks are needed next. The former claim — RoPE-aware prefetch worth +4 pts of hit rate, 93% → 97% cumulative — is withdrawn with the whole 72→80→87→93→97 ladder, which was never run as an ablation.

Distance decay is an upper bound, |⟨q_m, k_n⟩| ≤ B(|m − n|) — never ∝ cos((m − n)·θ). A position-window prefetch remains an open idea, not a validated technique: it has not been shown to beat the reuse-prediction approaches already shipping (CacheWise's tool-call-driven prediction, for instance), and it belongs in future work.

Rotary Encoding — what RoPE actually gives
RoPE encodes position by rotating Q/K in d/2 two-dimensional subspaces, each with its own frequency θ_i. The inner product is a sum over those d/2 frequency pairs, so it is not a single cosine of the distance and it does not fall off monotonically. The guarantee is an upper-bound envelope: |⟨q_m, k_n⟩| ≤ B(|m − n|) with B non-increasing. The curve at left sketches that envelope, not a measured attention trace.
Locality Bias — an ASSUMPTION of this model
This design assumes roughly 70% of attention mass falls within ±W positions of the query. That figure is not a universal property of transformers: it is model-, layer-, head- and workload-dependent, and it is stated here as an input assumption, not as an established fact or a measurement.
What it is NOT — ∝ cos((m − n)·θ)
The inner product is never proportional to a single cosine of the distance. Writing it that way turns an upper bound into a decay law, and a decay law reads like a prediction of which positions will be read. It is not one. A distant position may still attend strongly — the bound permits it — and nothing in the rotation says which blocks the next step will touch.

These three window sizes are not derived and were not selected by experiment. They are round powers of two spanning two orders of magnitude, chosen to show the shape of the trade-off; the coverage percentages beside them (~50% / ~75% / ~90%) are assumptions of the model, not a measured histogram, and W = 128 is labelled "balanced for Llama-70B" as a judgement call, not a fitted result.

What W controls. W sets the radius of the band [P − W, P + W] around a query position, and so the fraction of attention mass that falls inside it under the assumed distribution. It is a property of the bound, not a speculative-fetch budget.

No hit rate is attached to W. The claim that widening W lifts the hit rate — "the rung worth +4 pts, 93% → 97%" — is retracted, and no replacement figure is offered, because the ablation that would have produced one was never run. The tier latency this paragraph once priced the miss tail against is retracted too: no CXL hardware was measured. Tier latency is an assumption across 32 / 64 / 121 GB/s, under which a 5.00 MiB block fetch is 164 / 82 / 43 µs. What the tier buys is avoiding regeneration — forward compute for 16 tokens is 9 / 11 / 18 ms at 50 / 40 / 25% MFU, two to three orders of magnitude above the transfer — and no precise multiple is claimed.

Why the per-head framing does not save it. A head is not an eviction unit. Every head of every layer is required for every token, so evicting a head produces wrong outputs, not a cache miss. Per-head attention statistics are a scoring signal; the placed and moved unit is a (sequence, token block) of 16 tokens = 5.00 MiB. H2O and SnapKV evict (head, token) pairs — the token axis is not optional.

Where the leverage actually is. Not in the position window. At a 32 GiB budget, letting identity-keyed policy state survive HBM eviction is worth +34.15 pts to LFU (15.56 → 49.71) and +24.85 pts to EMA α=0.01 (31.54 → 56.39), while EMA α=0.15 is unchanged (−0.21 pts) — larger than the whole spread between policies, and a protocol result, testable with no CXL hardware.
